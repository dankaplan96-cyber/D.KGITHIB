# P116 — Overhead remarks for minor residents (the user's pick 23.11, "build now" 24.11)

The user's pick (status.md P116, lba3-audit/dialogue.md decision 3 (b)): minor residents say their one-line remark over
their heads, as LBA2's overhead lines do, instead of opening the dialogue box. Story and main characters keep the full
talk, with the talk camera and the name tag (both kept by the user).

Base: branch `ccr-c1c2c4b9-dep6ed` at 288f3c1b. Commits on this worktree's branch: 6e6cd32b, df41c68e, f179ac0f (+ this
doc). No new file the game loads (still 512 of 512); new files: `tests/overhead-remarks.mjs`, this doc,
`captures/p116/`. Evidence is **automated runtime** only (SwiftShader, headless Chromium, on a shared machine at load
~15 on 4 cores). Nobody played or listened.

## How talks open (what was read)
- `main.js tryInteract` → `story.talk(npc)` (story/index.js) → a long chain of talk handlers (ferry, oneplace, …,
  the chapter, shops, stands, ihomes, hlife, ulife, islife, purposes, ipeople, citizens, life, zlife, hostel, …).
- Every handler ends in `game.say(name, lines, done, speaker, color)`, directly or through `ChapterBase.chain`
  (story/base.js), which says one line per call and calls the next from `done`.
- So the change sits at the two ends: who the person is (data) and what the talk said (its `say` calls).

## The rule (game-wide, from data the game already has)
**Who is a minor resident** (`story/index.js npcDefs`: `def.minor` = the module's name):
- A person whose definition comes from the residents' modules: `citizens` (the streets' walkers of Citadel, Zeelich and
  Kesh; WC-3's people of the dig, the School, Lhar/the monastery and Zeelich's council quarter; the islands' people),
  `interiors` (the rooms' and both spaceports' people), `zlife` (Zeelich's street folk and its houses' people),
  `ihomes` (the island/Kesh/Hamalayi house keepers), `hlife` (the Hamalayi's people at home and the monk), `ulife` (the
  shield crew after the ending), `islife` (the islands' people indoors), `hostel` (the asylum hostel's workers).
- Never: anyone a chapter defines (asked first, so a chapter's own person keeps its definition: Ballastra's 17, Bram,
  Odile, Nell, Kell, Brel, Vesk…), Citadel's errand people (`life`), Corla's kiosk (`stands`), the side stories
  (ferry, oneplace, offwall, yearslate, quietchannel), any shopkeeper (`shops.js KEEPER`: Mr Bazoo, the pharmacist,
  Ines, Sefa, Tibbo; and Osk), anyone with `noTalk`, held or controlled by a chapter, or standing on the objective's
  star (`story.markers()`, as INTERACT-PICK reads them).

**When the talk is a remark** (`walkers.js Remarks.talk`): the talk's `say` calls are held while the story runs, then:
- exactly one `say`, one line, spoken by that person (not Twinsen, not a narrator);
- not the first of a chain (`chain` now passes `{ more }` as a sixth argument to `say`; `main.js say` ignores it);
- the talk changed no story flag (the line-set counters `*_n_*` aside), no coins and no life (Mama Pufa's soup, Sister
  Ione's healing, a clue's flag such as `hs_heard`: all keep the box);
- no scene, state change, cut-scene or fade started;
- the line is short: **at most 80 characters of its English source** (`spoken()`, so English and Hebrew decide the same)
  **and at most two sentences** ("..." / "…" end none).
- Anything else: the held calls are replayed unchanged, i.e. the box opens exactly as before (and the talk camera's
  two-shot is set first, as before).

**Item 3 of the brief (longer lines, chains):** a chain of 2+ lines keeps the full box from its first line. I did **not**
show "the first line only": deciding which rest is filler needs a person, line by line. Lines over the limit keep the box.

**Why two sentences, not one (a decision for the user, below).** Read from the residents' own text files (all 11, 1,046
lines): one sentence within 80 characters fits **67 lines (6 %)**; up to two sentences within 80 characters fits **409
(39 %)**. Our residents mostly speak in two short ones ("Two hundred steps to the quay and back, every day. Doctor's
orders."). With one sentence the feature would almost never be seen; in the chapter-3 Zeelich save no minor resident in
reach had a one-sentence line (run 3, B1). `?remarks=strict` keeps one sentence for the user's comparison.

## What it looks and feels like
- The bubble (`.remark`, css/style.css; one DOM element, `#floats`): over the head, with a tail; the resident's colour on
  its edge (LBA2's speaker colours); no name (the box keeps the name tag). `hud-lba` style: square, as the HUD.
- Size: `clamp(15px, 1.35vw, 20px) × --txs` (18.4 px at 1366, 15 px at 640; Hebrew +10 %); Text Size Large ×1.22,
  XL larger; at most `min(360px, 72vw)` wide; kept inside the screen.
- It follows the head every frame (the rig's height, as the dialogue's speaker marker); stays
  `max(2.6, 1.6 + 0.32 s a word)`, at most 7 s (×1.3 at the slow text speed); fades in its last 0.3 s.
- Closes: its time; E again (on that resident that is all the key does; on someone else it closes and that talk
  starts); walking 6.5 m away; any box, menu, scene or door. Then the talk's own end runs (`done`: talking off,
  release, its save), as when the box closes.
- No camera change (no two-shot, no talk shot), no control lock: the game stays in play.
- The resident stops and turns to him (its own handler's `engage`/`talking`, or the remark's when the handler has none)
  and **speaks with its voice** (`Babble.start` + the line's utterance; the mouth follows: `driveMouth`).
- Hebrew: only the text turns (the page's HE-LTR plaintext rule); the bubble and its tail stay where they are.
- Switch: `?remarks=box` is the old way (every talk in the box): the before pictures; `?remarks=strict`: one sentence.

## Decisions for the user
1. **How short is a remark?** (a) As built: ≤ 80 characters and ≤ 2 sentences (39 % of the residents' lines; most minor
   residents show remarks on some of their lines and the box on others). (b) One sentence only, as first briefed
   (`?remarks=strict`: 6 %; the feature is rarely seen). (c) All of a minor resident's single lines, any length, with a
   wider bubble (100 %; long lines over a head are hard to read at Far). *Recommendation: (a).*
2. **Mixed residents.** Under (a) the same resident answers over its head one time and in the box the next (its lines
   cycle). (a) Keep it. (b) Decide per person: a resident whose lines are all short gets remarks, everyone else the box
   (consistent per person; fewer remarks). (c) Shorten the long resident lines to fit (a text pass, EN+HE, for the
   dialogue owner). *Recommendation: (c) over time, (a) meanwhile.*
3. **Ballastra.** Its four background people (Sorrel, Brin, Ottie, Nuna: two lines each, by the chapter's state) are
   the chapter's cast, so they keep the box. (a) As built. (b) Tag those four as minor. *Recommendation: (a)*: their
   lines track the town's recovery, which is the chapter's story.
4. **The remark's look**: the speaker's colour on the edge, no name. (a) As built. (b) The name in small type over the
   line. *Recommendation: (a)* (LBA2 shows no name over a head).
