## THE CAMPAIGN

**1. Home — The water stops.**
Let the player spend a little time with Zoé and Arthur before asking them to save anything. Give Twinsen a household problem he can physically investigate: the kitchen tap is dry, but water is still knocking inside the wall. Arthur has put a cup underneath the wrong pipe. Move it, follow the sound into the yard, then descend to the well. The water below is warm; the growth on the masonry moves when the pump starts. Sendell's warning is brief. End with a practical reason to stay on Citadel: the next pump cycle is about to begin.

**2. Citadel — The wrong pipe.**
Build a proper investigation across the street, a furnished pump workshop and the underground junction. A repair worker opens the line: the public fountain starts, but the houses remain dry. The player sees the contradiction. Zoé tests the house taps while Twinsen follows the pressure underground. The wall diagram shows two pipes; the room has three. Let the player trace the newer pipe by its vibration and operate the bypass. A wrong setting sends water back to the fountain and can be corrected immediately.
When the house taps finally run, return control long enough for the player to see the street change: a shopkeeper washes the step; Arthur discovers he left the tap open. The repair also exposes a pressure pulse coming FROM the harbor connection. The pump plate names the workshop that built it on Zeelich. Now the player has earned the departure and knows whom to find.

**3. Zeelich — A town that needs the project.**
Give the arrival a playable route through a recognizable island district. Use the supplied LBA2 references to place the dock, changes in elevation, streets and recognizable landmarks. The player should be able to find the workshop from a dock worker's directions and a visible landmark. Build the doors, interiors, return routes and camera cutaways needed for that journey.
On arrival, a freight lift has stopped halfway up with a water cart on it. The lift operator will not release the brake: people are crossing below. Twinsen crosses to the upper landing, secures the cart and signals the operator. Vesk helps restart the motor. Ride up WITH the cart, unload it at the neighborhood cistern and see residents use the water. The mechanic who built Citadel's pump works here. She recognizes the pulse and lets Twinsen test it on her bench.
By the end, the player should know how to get from dock to workshop without a marker, remember at least two residents, and understand why decent people trust Vesk.

**4. The excavation — A test with a cost.**
At the existing research or desert site, give Twinsen and Vesk a chance to work well together. During a controlled test, the player operates a cutoff while Vesk reads the instruments. The drill stops; a second vibration arrives AFTER it stops. Let the player reproduce this once with the mechanism. Vesk diverts power to keep a settlement supplied, a decision that genuinely helps. He later submits a record showing only the first vibration. The player knows what is missing because they ran the test themselves.

**5. Hamalayi — The road under the ice.**
Make the disturbance change how the player moves through the region. A familiar crossing becomes unsafe. A maintenance worker knows an older route, but its supports carry the same pulse. Teach the player to read the movement of the supports and cross between surges; provide visual and sound cues. From the far side, open a safe way through for the residents. The discovery here is directional: the disturbance is traveling below the installations, beyond the excavation.

**6. The evidence — Vesk closes a door.**
Return to an established location. Vesk revokes access to the instruments after learning that Twinsen wants an independent reading. Keep the player's possessions and progress. The new obstacle is access, not an arbitrary inventory reset. The lift operator from chapter 3 can take Twinsen to the service landing because they repaired it together on the main route. Recover the unedited reading through maintenance equipment the player already understands. Show what Vesk changed and who authorized it. Give him one clear reason for doing it: an immediate shutdown would cut the water supply before a bypass exists.

**7. The evacuation — Zoé finds the omission.**
Zoé compares the evacuation schedule with the actual transport route. An inhabited lower district has no pickup. Let the player arrive in time to see a vehicle pass its closed gate with empty seats. Zoé takes charge of getting people to the landing; Twinsen must reopen a usable route through infrastructure he knows. The player sees residents from chapter 3 board. This is a main-story event: no optional friendship or collectible may be required to save them.

**8. Below the shell — Someone answers.**
Slow the pace through discovery rather than exposition. A mechanism responds to a signal Twinsen did not send. Let the player repeat a short pattern, receive a different reply and use it to navigate. If the approved story includes a guardian from another world, make their first intervention helpful: they hold a failing passage open. Later, the same intelligence seals a door to prevent someone from leaving "for their protection." Show the action before explaining the motive. Keep Sendell distinct and preserve her role.

**9. The failed shutdown — The player learns why stopping is not enough.**
Let the player perform the shutdown everyone has been arguing about. Give immediate, readable feedback: the drill winds down, but the underground pulse continues and the town's supply starts losing pressure. The shutdown was a reasonable decision; its result reveals something new. Baldino can isolate the controls from the supply, but his device can hold only one branch steady at a time. Let the player try the prototype on a small circuit and see that limitation.

**10. The network — Allies take responsibility.**
Avoid a tour to collect three identical parts. Revisit places whose people and machinery the player already knows, each with a different job. Zoé organizes the evacuation route; the Zeelich mechanic takes over the local bypass; Twinsen restores the damaged connection at the hardest station. Arthur notices an irregular interval because he recognizes the knocking sound from home. His observation helps; an adult verifies it on the gauge. Make allies visibly take over their stations so the player understands how one person can now reach the core.

**11. The deep installation — Reach the controls.**
Build one sustained playable sequence from established mechanics. Read the pulse to cross moving machinery, use a banked magic-ball throw to reach a switch beyond a barrier, then open a route back from the far side. Teach or refresh any required skill on the main path, with a safe attempt before danger. Let the player hear allies maintaining their stations. Use combat where an opponent has a reason to hold a position. Include a checkpoint before the confrontation.

**12. The decision — Keep the water running.**
Make the final action specific: separate the system that keeps people alive from the controls being used to confine or overrule them. Twinsen reroutes one branch, gets confirmation from the mechanic, releases the central lock and gives Zoé the signal. Vesk must make a visible choice and face consequences for the falsified record and selective evacuation. If the guardian is present, resolve their conflict separately; possession must not excuse Vesk's decisions. Finish the central conflict here.

**Playable aftermath.**
Return to the kitchen. Let the player turn the tap themselves. The water runs cold. Arthur has left the cup underneath, just in case. Give the player a moment with Zoé before the credits. No speech about destiny is needed.

## BALANCE EVERY CHAPTER
Aim for broadly comparable substance across ALL core chapters. First measure the existing main route, excluding side quests, idle time, loading and repeated failures. Track automated traversal separately from first-play estimates. Use the median core chapter as a provisional reference; investigate chapters shorter than 75% or longer than 125% of it. If the entire campaign is thin, the median is not a sufficient target. The aftermath can be brief.

For a chapter that feels short, identify the missing playable step. Did the player discover the problem, test an idea and see the result, or did an NPC do all three in a conversation? Add the missing action. For a chapter that feels easy, ask the player to apply something learned earlier in a new situation, with readable feedback and a quick retry. Avoid padding through long walks, enemy health, mandatory side quests or repeated switches. Keep the roughly 15-hour adventure goal separate from measured main-route time; never claim hours that have not been demonstrated.

- Keep player-facing text in short, natural English. Prefer a pipe that rattles, a gate that stays shut or a person who returns to work over a journal entry explaining the same thing.
- Use existing locations, residents and machinery wherever practical. Add life through purposeful actions and changed states; preserve the Vega 8 performance budget.
- Test fresh saves, existing saves, chapter boundaries, leaving mid-task, failure and retry, saving after a partial repair, and returning after completion. Verify that doors, lifts, NPCs and quest flags agree after reload.
