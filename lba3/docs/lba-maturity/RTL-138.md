## P138: in Hebrew the speaker's name comes first, on the right

### Cause
`#cine-sub` (every story film's subtitle line) is `<b>name</b> <span>words</span>`. HE-LTR's rule
`:where(:root[lang=he]) :where(:not([dir])) { unicode-bidi: plaintext }` reached the subtitle box itself, not only
its children. With `plaintext` the box takes its direction from its first strong letter, but every letter sits inside
an isolated child (the `<b>` and the `<span>` are isolates under the same rule), so no letter is found and the
paragraph falls back to left to right. That overrode the `direction: rtl` meant for it (style.css, "the name opens
the Hebrew sentence"). The result: the name sat at the left end of the line, so it was read last. In a two-line
subtitle the words wrapped to the left of the name on line 1 and went on below, so the name ended up mid-text. The
local agent's analysis says the same and was confirmed here: the measured computed `unicode-bidi` of `#cine-sub` was
`plaintext` and the name was on the left.

### Fix (css/style.css, HE-LTR block)
- `:root[lang=he] #cine-sub, :root[lang=he] #omtune .omline { direction: rtl; unicode-bidi: isolate; }`. The box is
  an isolate of its own, so its rtl holds. The name is on the right and the words start just left of it. On two
  lines the name is on line 1 at the right and line 2 has words only. One CSS rule covers every writer of the line:
  the opening (`story/opening.js`, both cuts), the ending film (`story/ending.js`), the ferry film (`story/ferry.js`),
  the radio film (`story/oneplace.js`), the travel films (`travelfilm.js`, through `engine/cinematic.js`) and
  chapter 1's ferry narrator (`story/chapter1.js narr`, which writes `#cine-sub` directly). A `dir` set in
  cinematic.js would have missed that last one.
- The radio tuning's line (`#omtune .omline`, oneplace.js) already read right to left, because its words are a bare
  text node, so `plaintext` found the Hebrew. By the same rule, a line starting with a Latin word would have turned
  it left to right. That is reasoning, not measured; the test's number-first line read right to left before the
  change too. It gets the same explicit rule.
- Dialogue box name tag: in Hebrew it now sits at the top right, over where the Hebrew line starts:
  `:root[lang=he] #dialog .name { inset-inline-start: auto; inset-inline-end: 20px; }`. This is the user's pick,
  passed on by the local agent through the coordinator. The box, its ▼, the key hint and the rest of HE-LTR are as
  in English. `tests/hebrew.mjs`'s Arthur check was changed to match (it asserted the tag on the left); that suite
  was not re-run.
- English is unchanged; every rule is under `:root[lang=he]`.

The same cause applies wherever a Hebrew block holds its letters only in inline isolated children. The objective
panel (`<b>goal</b><span class="s">`, HUD and pause) is not affected: both children are `display: block`, so each is
its own paragraph, and hebrew.mjs measures it right to left. Other panels were not swept by measurement; the named
surfaces above were.

The dialogue tag change reverses one item of HE-LTR (the user's 17.11: "the side of a name tag as in English"). It
came to this worker second-hand (the local agent's report of the user's pick, through the coordinator), so it is
listed as a visible change for the user to confirm. To undo it, remove the one `:root[lang=he] #dialog .name` rule in
css/style.css and revert hebrew.mjs's Arthur check. HE-LTR.md's lines about the tag describe the old state.

## P139b: the black square on switching to Aggressive (F3)

### In the recordings: real, at two frame-exact places
The local agent's recorder (`captures/hw-75/trailer.mjs`) draws the game's WebGL canvas onto a 1280×720 2D canvas
every frame and records that canvas with Chrome's MediaRecorder (H.264, a real GPU, Windows). It pauses the recorder
(`rec.cut()`) across each mode switch and resumes it (`rec.uncut()`) 0.4 s later. Both published trailers were
scanned frame by frame (`-vsync passthrough`) with the same rule the test below uses: a block of cells near black
for one frame, between two frames where those cells are not.
- `awakening-trailer.mp4`: one hit, frame 975 (pts 33.42 s). A black rectangle (565..1280, 0..604) covers the top
  right. The "Discreet" label is up: it is 6 frames after the resume that follows the F4 switch, as the camera starts
  to turn. The frame is a P-frame of 3,424 bytes, smaller than its neighbours (5.4–7.6 KB), and the visible part
  around it is heavily blocky.
- `awakening-trailer-20s.mp4`: one hit, frame 379 (pts 13.46 s). The black rectangle is (736..1280, 0..560), exactly
  on the 16-px macroblock grid on all four sides. The label is again "Discreet", 7 frames after the resume. The
  frame is an I-frame of only 7,683 bytes; the take's other I-frame is 37 KB and the P-frames next to it are about
  10 KB.
- The local agent's own sighting (an earlier take, F3, 30.5 s) is the same pattern at another switch.

One more observation cuts the other way. In both flash frames the visible part shows a view off the camera's path:
it looks further back than both neighbouring frames. Its mean grey difference to the frame before and the frame
after is about 19–24, against 13–16 between ordinary neighbouring frames there. An I-frame is encoded from what the
canvas held at that moment, so the 20 s take's source frame was itself different. That fits a one-frame camera pop
(for example the camera backing into something dark close to the lens) as well as a capture fault. Both trailers
come from the same script at the same spot and step (F4, then A held), and both show it once at the same moment.

All three sit a few frames after a resume that follows a mode switch. In each take only one of the three switches showed
it. The flash frames are badly undersized encodes, and the 20 s one has macroblock-aligned edges. Those facts point to
the recording's H.264 encode, but the off-path view above points to something in the picture itself. Undecided: only a
lossless capture on the real GPU can settle it (below).

### In the game: not reproduced (automated, SwiftShader)
`tests/mode-switch-frames.mjs` puts Twinsen on Citadel with Modern keys, as recorded. The spot is camcitadel's spot
12 (-32.6, -38), which turned out to be the foot of a stone stair by the cliff, not the square. It presses real keys
through the game's input and samples the game's canvas right after every drawn frame on a 48×27 brightness grid.
Each variant is a fresh page, so the first switch is a first use.
- A, fixed stepping: F3 first, then F1–F4 three times. 13 presses, all switched; 140 drawn frames; no one-frame dark
  block, no black frame. The first F3 and its 9 following frames are on the sheet: unchanged picture, no square.
- B, the game's own loop (requestAnimationFrame, `testFreeze` off), 960×540: F3 first, then F1–F4 three times. 13
  presses, all switched; each press was followed by 9–11 drawn frames; 182 frames in all; no dark block, no black
  frame. The loop ran at about 0.2 fps on this loaded machine, so this covers the first frames after each switch, not
  real timing.
- C, the own loop: Ctrl held + F3 first, then F1, a Ctrl tap (the menu opened and closed), F3, F1, F4. 6 presses, all
  switched; 83 frames; no dark block outside the behaviour menu's own frames (2), no black frame. (A first run at
  1366×768, with a fixed 0.9 s between presses, gave only about 51 and 27 frames for B and C. It found the same:
  nothing outside the menu's frames.)
- D, the recording's own sequence (trailer.mjs section 7) on the fixed clock at 960×540, Modern, the Far camera.
  Twinsen starts beside the two residents nearest the market point the trailer walked to (Pippa at -46.2, -0.9 and Dot
  at -42, -5.8). The keys: F2, W 1.4 s and Space; F3 and Space twice; F4, then A held 2 s. The F4 + A part was drawn,
  81 frames a spot (162 in all): no dark block, no black frame. The camera was checked on every frame by camwalk.js:
  never inside something solid, never behind a drawn face, never cut by the near plane. Its turn was at most
  4°/frame, the arm at least 10.75 m, with no jump in the arm. The trailer's exact spot is unknown (it talked to
  whoever was nearest in its save), so this is near it, not on it.
- Detector check: in C, the behaviour menu's own frames show as dark blocks (its four portraits are drawn into the
  canvas on near-black boxes, centred; the frame and labels around them are the page's). A canvas-only capture shows
  the menu as dark squares in the middle, but only while Ctrl is held. That does not match a plain F3 and a large
  square at the middle-left or top right, so it is listed, not counted.
- Limits: fixed stepping skips the warm-up paths gated by `testFreeze` (`main.js` compile and warm jobs, `gfxHold`).
  The live loop ran slowly on this shared machine (load about 20). SwiftShader is not the recorder's GPU or encoder.

### What was not done
No prewarm or other change was made. Nothing in the game was found to fix, and a speculative change labelled a fix
would hide the question. Proposed next step (for the local agent, real GPU): record the F3/F4 switch and the turn
after it as a lossless sequence (`toDataURL` of the game canvas each frame, or OBS at constant quality) rather than
MediaRecorder with pause/resume. If the square appears in a lossless frame it is the game's; if not, it is the encode.
